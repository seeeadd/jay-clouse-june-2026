Roadmap 1 · Just Starting
From promise to founding launch.
You have an audience and an idea. You don't have a membership problem yet. You have a promise problem and a launch problem. Ninety days solves both and opens the doors with real founding members. The order matters: concept, then pre-launch, then launch. Don't skip ahead.

Phase 1 · Days 1–30Lock the concept

GoalA Promise so clear a stranger could read it and know what they'd become, and proof your audience actually wants it.

  • Write your Promise in one sentence. One verb, one outcome, a recurring frame. "A community for [niche]" is a category, not a promise. Rewrite it until it names what a member becomes, not what room they're in.
  • Send the three-question survey to your audience this week. What they want help with, what they've tried that didn't stick, whether they'd take a five-minute call. Even three replies beats three months of guessing.
  • Book three of those five-minute calls. Listen for the exact words people use for their problem. Those words become your page later.
  • Decide what this is NOT. Not a course, not a content vault. Shared, not parallel. Members do things together or it isn't a membership.
  • Choose your smallest viable room and set that as your launch target. Belonging happens in rooms of eight to fifteen. Design for that, not for scale.
Done whenYou can say your Promise in one sentence and at least five people have told you, in their words, they'd pay for it.
You'll know it's working whenPeople you didn't prompt start describing their problem back to you in your Promise language.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60Pre-launch

GoalA real waitlist, a price built for renewal, and a first-week experience designed before anyone's inside.

  • Set your price for renewal, not the launch spike. The number a member happily pays in month seven. Your Pricing tool puts the target at . Work backward from members × price, not from hope.
  • Open a waitlist with the Promise on it. Real names, real emails. Aim for a number that makes a founding launch feel alive, not empty.
  • Map a new member's first seven days before you build anything. First action, first win, first person they meet. "Here's your login, welcome" is how memberships quietly die.
  • Design your one recurring ritual. The thing members can count on every week. Cadence beats volume. Pick something you can sustain for a year.
  • Pick the platform last. Platform paralysis is usually an authority question in a tech costume. Once the Promise and the first-week map are clear, the tool decision gets easy.
Done whenThe waitlist is live and filling, the price is set, and the first-seven-days map is written start to finish.
You'll know it's working whenA waitlist signup comes from a share you didn't make. Someone passed it on.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90Founding launch

GoalDoors open, founding members in, the first ritual running.

  • Launch to the waitlist as founding members. Small on purpose, clear promise, real close date. No urgency theater. The close date is real, not manufactured.
  • Onboard every single member through the first-seven-days map. Not "most." Every one. Someone notices when a new member goes quiet.
  • Run the first recurring ritual in week one, then repeat it before you add anything new. One thing they can count on outweighs five they can't.
  • Engineer the first member-to-member connection on purpose. A pairing, an intro thread, a small group. They joined because of you; they stay because of each other.
  • Day 90: audit yourself against this page. What shipped, what drifted, what members actually said. That's the real test of the first quarter.
Done whenThe founding cohort is in, onboarded, and the ritual has run at least twice.
You'll know it's working whenA member shows up for the ritual without a reminder. The room has its own pulse.

Roadmap 2 · Recently Launched
Stop the leak. Re-found the room.
You launched. The launch high faded. That's design, not failure. Months four to six are won with mechanics, not more content. Your ninety days runs a cycle: diagnose, rebuild, re-found. You're not relaunching from scratch. You're fixing the leaks and re-founding the room around what actually makes people stay.

Phase 1 · Days 1–30Diagnose

GoalKnow exactly where members are leaking and which mechanic is the cause, before you build anything new.

  • Start with your weakest mechanic: . Fix order matters. Don't polish a strong mechanic while the weak one leaks members.
  • Re-test your Promise against reality. Does a member's first week match what the page promised? If not, the page is writing checks the room doesn't cash.
  • Talk to three members who renewed and two who left. Ask what the value actually was. Month-six members say different things than launch-week members. Their words are the diagnosis.
  • Count true activity, not vanity. Who showed up twice in the last thirty days. That's your real room. Everything else is a mailing list.
  • Map where new members go quiet in their first week. That drop-off point is your biggest leak. Mark it.
Done whenYou can name your weakest mechanic, your first-week drop-off point, and the gap between promise and experience.
You'll know it's working whenThe churned-member conversations keep surfacing the same one or two reasons. You've found the pattern.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60Rebuild

GoalFix the leaks at the structural level: onboarding, friend-making, and what you ship.

  • Kill the hamster wheel. List everything you ship. Cut the bottom half. Year two doesn't renew on fresh content. It renews on identity.
  • Rebuild onboarding as a map, not a sandbox. First action, first win, first friend, inside seven days, for every join, automatically.
  • Install one friend-making structure. Small-group pairings, a standing thread, a mastermind pod. Members stay for each other.
  • Re-price if the math says so, for renewal, not optics. Your Pricing tool's target: . If the current price can't get there with a room you can actually serve, the price is the problem.
  • Set a founder cadence you can hold for a year. Speed-to-response kills memberships quietly. Pick a rhythm and keep it.
Done whenThe new onboarding is live, the bottom-half content is cut, and one friend-making structure is running.
You'll know it's working whenA new member hits their first win inside week one without you personally walking them there.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90Re-found

GoalRelaunch the room to your existing members and lock in the new shape.

  • Re-onboard your existing members like a relaunch. The new map, the new ritual, said out loud: "here's what this room is now." Members forgive drift; they don't forgive silence about it.
  • Make founder presence sustainable. The cadence you set in the rebuild, now a habit, not a sprint.
  • Watch renewals cohort by cohort, not as one churn number. Month-six renewal is the real test, not launch week.
  • Double down on the one structure that's working. Quietly retire what isn't. Evidence over attachment.
  • Day 90: audit against this page. The drift either stopped or it didn't. Both are data you can act on.
Done whenExisting members have been re-onboarded into the new shape and you're tracking renewal by cohort.
You'll know it's working whenA member who was drifting re-engages after the re-onboarding. The room pulled them back.

Roadmap 3 · Scaling
Design beats more of you.
It works. The question is what compounds without more of you, because more of you is not a strategy. Ninety days moves the membership from depending on your hours to running on design. Audit the load, delegate to design, compound. The goal at day 90 isn't more revenue this month. It's a room that grows when you're not in it.

Phase 1 · Days 1–30Audit the load

GoalSee exactly where the membership leans on you, and find the room inside the room.

  • Write down every hour the membership takes from you in a typical week. Mark what only you can do. Everything else is a design problem, not a workload problem.
  • Score the six mechanics as systems, not vibes. is where the structure leans on you hardest. That's the first redesign.
  • Find your most active eight to fifteen members. They're not your best customers. They're your next layer of structure.
  • Stop shipping the bottom half of what you produce. A membership is a resource, not a task. That applies to you too.
  • Pick the one ritual that, if members ran it, would free the most of your time. That's your first hand-off candidate.
Done whenYou have a written list of your weekly hours, what only you can do, and your top eight to fifteen members named.
You'll know it's working whenYou spot at least two rituals that don't actually need you in the room.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60Delegate to design

GoalHand rituals to members and document the mechanics so they run without you.

  • Hand one ritual to members to run. The strongest memberships reach the point where members run rooms the founder just visits. That's friend-making in its year-three form: the membership becomes infrastructure, not content.
  • Document the mechanics. Onboarding, cadence, rituals, written so they run without you in the room. The soul stays yours. The mechanics shouldn't need you.
  • Name a second layer of leaders from your active eight to fifteen. Brief them. Then get out of the way, publicly, so the room knows it's real.
  • Re-check pricing against what the room delivers now. Target from your Pricing tool: . Mature rooms usually undercharge out of habit.
  • Build one member-led structure that creates belonging without you. A chapter, a pod, a recurring member-hosted session.
Done whenAt least one ritual is member-run, the core mechanics are documented, and a second leadership layer exists.
You'll know it's working whenA member-run session happens and is good. And you didn't have to be there.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90Compound

GoalShift your time from production to presence and design for year-two renewal.

  • Move your reclaimed hours from making things to being in the room where it counts. Fewer deliverables, more presence.
  • Design the year-two renewal around identity, not novelty. What does a member become by staying, and where do they see that progress?
  • Let the room recruit. Referral happens when belonging is real. Measure it, don't force it.
  • Set the metric that matters now. Not new content shipped. Member-to-member activity and renewal rate.
  • Day 90: audit against this page, specifically the weekly-hours number from day one. It should have moved.
Done whenYour founder hours are down, member-led structures are carrying the room, and you're tracking renewal and member activity over output.
You'll know it's working whenThe room runs a full week at full energy on a week you were barely in it.

Roadmap 4 · Pivoting From Courses
Change the front door, keep the house.
The course math worked. The model is what's tired: transactional, launch to launch, no compounding relationship. You're not abandoning the courses. You're changing what the front door is. Ninety days builds the bridge: the room becomes the product, the courses become benefits inside it.

Phase 1 · Days 1–30The bridge plan

GoalA recurring Promise and a clear map of which assets move inside the room.

  • Write the recurring Promise. What a member becomes over twelve months, not what they finish in a six-week course. Recurring revenue requires recurring value.
  • Map your assets. Which courses become benefits inside the room, which get sunset. The room is the product. The courses are no longer the front door.
  • Survey your buyer list with the three questions. Your course buyers are the warmest waitlist you'll ever have. Treat them like one.
  • Price for staying, not the drop. Launch-spike pricing is the course habit talking. Sanity-check against your Pricing tool's .
  • Decide your bridge. How the course revenue keeps running while you build the recurring engine, so you're not betting the business on month one.
Done whenThe recurring Promise is written, the asset map is done (keep, fold, sunset), and your buyer-list waitlist has started.
You'll know it's working whenPast buyers reply to your survey saying they'd want an ongoing room, not just another course.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60The room becomes the front door

GoalOpen the smallest viable room with founding members pulled from your buyer list.

  • Build the smallest viable room and invite past buyers first. Founding members, capped on purpose, with a real close date.
  • Design week one around doing, not consuming. Your buyers have already consumed plenty. The scarcity is doing alongside other people: belonging and becoming, not another module.
  • Fold the kept courses in as member benefits. Positioned as part of the room, not the reason to join.
  • Set a cadence you can keep while the course business still runs. Async by default. A resource, not a task, for them and for you.
  • Keep one launch muscle. A founding window with a clear close. After it closes, no theater.
Done whenThe room is open, founding members from your buyer list are in, and the kept courses live inside it as benefits.
You'll know it's working whenA past course buyer joins the room and says it's what they actually wanted from the courses.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90First renewal cycle

GoalProve the recurring engine is real and start retiring the old model deliberately.

  • Ship the first month-three checkpoint where members see their own progress. Members renew on visible progress. Build the moment they feel it.
  • Watch the bridge-year math honestly. The stretch between the old model and the new one feels longest from the inside. The curve climbs before the feeling does. That gap is normal. It is not a verdict.
  • Sunset or fold each remaining course deliberately. Every course either feeds the room or distracts from it. Decide one by one.
  • Run your first renewal conversation with founding members. What made them stay, what almost made them leave. That's your retention blueprint.
  • Day 90: audit against this page. The question isn't "did it replace course revenue yet." It's "is the recurring engine real and growing."
Done whenThe first renewal cycle has run, the month-three checkpoint shipped, and every course has a deliberate keep, fold, or sunset status.
You'll know it's working whenA founding member renews. The first recurring dollar that didn't require a new launch.
